## 1. Executive findings

**The corpus invalidates the Phase 3.2 wine-role model. It does not
invalidate the rest.**

What Phase 3.2 got wrong is the wine role. It modelled a role as one
value, GRAND_VIN, SECOND_WINE, OTHER_CUVEE or UNKNOWN, scoped by a colour
range. The estates' own words show four separate things folded into
that value:

1. **Rank, which is a claim with a claimant.**
   - Batailley names a *third* wine, and so does Caillou.
   - Le Rey states that its two wines are *not* first and second.
   - Mangot's cuvée is "second wine as far as the classification
     authorities are concerned … in truth the superior cuvée".
   - Corbin made "no second wine this year".
2. **Wine type, independent of rank.** Caillou's Myosotis is "notre grand
   vin blanc sec", its Murets blanc is "Second Vin blanc sec", and Les
   Tourelles is "notre troisième vin", a sweet wine.
3. **How a wine is made or selected**: plot selection, young vines, old
   vines. Mostly these are winemaking statements, occasionally the
   defining trait of a wine (Lacroix-Martillac, "sélection parcellaire").
   Never a rank.
4. **The relationship to the estate**, which is not always "produced by".
   - Les Terrasses is made from the estate's vines *and* "created and
     orchestrated by the Vignobles K team".
   - Château Langlet is sold on Latour-Martillac's site but is "property
     of the Jean Kressmann family", a different estate.

**The revision.** One concept becomes four (§17):

- Wine.type;
- RankAssertion (tier, scope, claimant, validity);
- WineDescriptor;
- EstateWineRelationship.kind.

Smaller additions come from the corpus too:

- ranges above the first wine (ABOVE_FIRST) and explicit non-ranking
  (UNRANKED);
- vintage-scoped absences;
- market aliases;
- document dates as statement anchors;
- candidate relations between language versions and revisions of a
  document;
- source–estate relationships;
- an ANNULLED classification edition;
- extraction status for documents waiting on OCR.

**What held.** The rest of Phase 3.2 survives the corpus:

- identity and legacy maps;
- validity on two axes;
- the artifact/placement split;
- observations, reconciliation and canonical projection;
- classification and appellation on the Wine (re-tested in §8, confirmed).

**Phase 2 proposals.**

- Echo is ready for decision.
- Murets blanc is ready.
- Terrasses is ready for vintages 2016–2019; its role from 2020 still
  needs evidence.

**Readiness.** All twenty Phase 4 requirements are met by the revised
model, each shown on a real case (§19).

## 3. Wine model review

Phase 3.2: **RoleAssignment {role ∈ GRAND_VIN | SECOND_WINE | OTHER_CUVEE
| UNKNOWN, range ∈ RED | DRY_WHITE | SWEET_WINE | ROSE | SPARKLING |
UNKNOWN}**, on the estate–wine relationship.

What the corpus needs, dimension by dimension:

| Dimension | Evidence | Phase 3.2 | Phase 3.4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | Desmirail: "Initial de Desmirail, aussi distribué sous le nom de Origine de Desmirail"; Haut-Bailly: "La Parde de Haut-Bailly … se voit renommer" (Haut-Bailly II) | Wine + NameRecord | **retained**, plus NameRecord `context` and MARKET_ALIAS |
| Type | Caillou's range; Climens' Asphodèle (dry white, AOC Bordeaux) beside its Barsac; Latour-Martillac "Le Rosé, rosé de pressée" | `range` inside the role | **split out:** Wine.type |
| Rank | third wines, superior cuvées, explicit non-ranking, claimant disagreement (§4) | `role` | **renamed and extended:** RankAssertion {tier, scope, claimant} |
| Making / selection | plot selection 654 artifacts / 71 domains, young vines 701 / 77, old vines 636 / 76 | not modelled | **added:** WineDescriptor |
| Estate relationship | §6 | one link, status only | **extended:** EstateWineRelationship.kind |
| History | Echo/Averous, La Parde, Opalie's label change | validity, transitions | **retained**, with evidence criteria (§7) |

**Why not simpler.** A single "role" cannot say all of the following:

- Myosotis is FIRST *among dry whites*, while Château Caillou is FIRST
  overall;
- one cuvée is SECOND to the classification body and ABOVE_FIRST to the
  press;
- Le Rey's wines are deliberately unranked;
- Lacroix-Martillac is a plot selection with no rank claimed.

Each simplification tried loses a case the corpus actually contains.

**Why not more detailed.** The corpus gives no evidence for:

- numeric ranks beyond third;
- ranks per market;
- several simultaneous producer ranks for one wine.

None of these is modelled.

**Results:**

- **Distinct concepts:** FIRST, SECOND, THIRD, ABOVE_FIRST, UNRANKED.
- **Linguistic variants:** grand vin / premier vin / first wine;
  second vin / second wine / second label / 2nd vin / Zweitwein / petit
  frère.
- **Contextual:** cuvée prestige. It becomes ABOVE_FIRST only where a
  source places it above.
- **Marketing, not hierarchy:** premium, limited edition, special cuvée.
- **Ambiguous:** "grand vin" as praise, "premier vin" in narrative.
- **Explicitly non-hierarchical:** Le Rey.

**Ranks have claimants.** Mangot is the decisive case: a producer's joke,
the classification authorities, and the press place one cuvée
differently. So RankAssertion carries a **claimant**, and a wine can hold
several assertions at once, never reconciled into one silently.

## 5. Wine type analysis

**Type is independent of rank.**

- Caillou alone has FIRST overall (the Barsac), FIRST among dry whites
  (Myosotis), SECOND among dry whites (Murets blanc) and THIRD (Les
  Tourelles, sweet).
- "Grand vin blanc sec" occurs 94 times and "blanc sec" 636: ranks
  *within* a type are normal.

**The vocabulary:** RED, WHITE_DRY, WHITE_SWEET, ROSE, SPARKLING, OTHER,
UNKNOWN. The corpus supports each:

- rosé "de pressée" (Latour-Martillac);
- crémant (Caillou's Bulles);
- "OTHER" for the rare fortified or beverage items on group sites.

**A wine's type does not change in the corpus.** It stays an attribute of
the Wine. A change of type would be a new Wine.

**A rank's scope** is either the estate's whole range or one type's range
(RankScope + scope_type).

## 6. Estate–wine relationship analysis

| Relationship kind | Evidence | Pattern count (upper bound) |
|---|---|---|
| PRODUCED_BY | the default on estates' own pages ("notre troisième vin") | — |
| FROM_ESTATE_VINEYARD | "issu des jeunes vignes", "made from some of the younger vines planted on the terrasses of the estate" | 280 / 219 artifacts / 51 domains |
| MADE_BY_GROUP_TEAM | Vignobles K: "Créée et orchestrée par l'équipe des Vignobles K, la cuvée Les Terrasses …"; the same words for Les Tours de Belcier | group wording on group sites |
| SOLD_BY | Barton & Guestier's site carries Château Magnol (a Cru Bourgeois) | brand/négoce wording, noisy |
| SAME_OWNER_OTHER_PROPERTY | Latour-Martillac's press kit: "Château Langlet (rouge & blanc), propriété de la famille Jean Kressmann depuis 1999" | "our other estates" 456 artifacts / 40 domains |
| ASSOCIATED_UNSPECIFIED | a wine mentioned with no stated link | |
| historical | handled by validity (Haut-Bages Averous) | "formerly"/"anciennement" 859 artifacts |

**Conclusions.**

- **A wine mentioned on an estate's website is not thereby the estate's
  wine.** EstateWineRelationship gets a **kind**.
- **Several kinds can hold at once,** one record each, each with its own
  validity and provenance. Les Terrasses is FROM_ESTATE_VINEYARD and
  MADE_BY_GROUP_TEAM.
- **Groups are Parties linked by Ownership; group sites are Sources**
  (§13).

**The gap the corpus exposed is anchoring.** "Today", "now" and
"currently" appear in 4,843 artifacts. Such a statement is true *as of
the document's date*, and that date is usually knowable:

- PDF metadata dates in 6,683 of 7,197 PDFs;
- upload-path dates in 5,074;
- HTTP Last-Modified on 11,471 fetches.

So the model now separates three dates:

- **when the document speaks** (ArtifactDate, carried into
  Observation.asserted_at);
- **when the fact holds** (the validity of the state record);
- **when it was retrieved** (Placement).

No date is invented: an unknown anchor is UNKNOWN.

## 8. Classification and appellation review

**Classification stays on the Wine.**

- **Graves by colour.** Graves classifies by colour, and the estates say
  so: "Château Bouscaut Rouge Grand Cru Classé de Graves" and "Château
  Bouscaut Blanc Grand Cru Classé de Graves"; "Château Olivier Rouge 2018
  Grand Cru Classé de Graves". There are 189 artifacts on 8 domains with
  colour-specific classification wording.
- **Crus Artisans by appellation.** Moutte Blanc is classified twice
  (Phase 3.0).
- **Other wines are not classified with their estate.** Mangot's cuvée is
  "second wine as far as the classification authorities are concerned".

A classification of the estate is a derived view: the estate of a
classified wine. EstateWineRelationship is not the right anchor, because
classification survives changes of relationship.

**Claims are split from memberships.** Sites carry 1,851 classification
observations on 84 domains, for example "Cru Bourgeois depuis 2013,
confirmé en 2018" (Prieuré de Beyzac). These are the estate's claims.

- They stay Observations (class B).
- Authoritative ClassificationMembership comes from the Phase 3.0 sources.

The two are reconciled, not merged.

**Appellation stays on the Wine.** Of 120 sites stating an appellation,
57 state two or more recurring values. The values are noisy (shop text),
but the genuine cases are clear:

- Climens: "1er Cru Barsac" for the sweet wine, "Bordeaux Blanc Sec" for
  Asphodèle;
- Grand-Brun: "AOC Haut-Médoc Cru Artisan" beside "AOC Bordeaux
  Supérieur";
- Barateau: Haut-Médoc and Listrac;
- Caillou: Murets rouge Bordeaux Supérieur, Murets blanc AOC Bordeaux.

## 10. Canonical-versus-observation analysis

| Class | Categories | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| **A — core canonical** | appellation (wine); classification memberships (wine); vineyard area (estate, as_of); planting composition, planting density and average vine age (estate, as_of); blend (vintage); alcohol, pH, total acidity (with unit), residual sugar (vintage); harvest dates and yield (vintage); ageing months and new-oak share (vintage); owner (Ownership); rank (RankAssertion); wine type | recurring on 100+ sites, mostly numeric or short, stable meaning, already partly in the 105 fields |
| **B — structured observation** | classification claims; vintage-conditions narrative; winemaking and viticulture practice; élevage description; soil and terroir text; bottling and production; service, pairing and ageing text; tasting text per source; sustainability claims; people and staff; legal entity | recurring but variable in meaning or wording; keep structured, decide later |
| **C — source-specific structured** | press scores quoted by an estate; contact data of a source; technical-sheet fields particular to a DocumentFamily (IPT, VA, SO₂, Chinese-only labels) | meaningful inside one source or family |
| **D — raw evidence only** | commercial (prices, shop mechanics); tourism; privacy text | volatile or non-domain |
| **E — derived** | single variety (from the blend); estate-level classification view; drinking-window years parsed from the window text; the "second wine" list per estate (from RankAssertions) | computed, never stored independently |
| **F — needs study** | history as dated events (abundant in prose, needs event extraction); the 48,169 unclassified observations | insufficient structure today |

**Phase 4 projects only class A.** Everything else stays observations,
retrievable but not promoted.

## 11. Fiche-family review

Sheet-like PDFs (five or more labels each, 4,462 documents) were grouped
per site by label-set similarity (Jaccard ≥ 0.5).

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Families | 849 |
| Families of three or more sheets | 278 |
| Largest families | 211, 107 and 104 sheets |
| Domains with a family of three or more | 92 |
| Domains with several families | 69 (per wine, per period, or per language) |
| Families spanning languages | 27 |
| Label sets shared between domains | none |

**Conclusion.** A family is **per source, often several per source,
sometimes multilingual, and never cross-estate**. The earlier
FicheFamily → FicheInstance idea survives as **DocumentFamily**, but as
a *derived* extraction aid attached to a Source. It is not a domain
entity, and nothing canonical depends on it. A family is evidence that
its members share a layout, never that they share a wine or a vintage.

## 12. Multilingual review

**The corpus is multilingual:**

| Language | Artifacts |
|---|---|
| French | 16,707 |
| English | 7,880 |
| Chinese | 1,053 |
| German | 250 |
| Japanese | 239 |

**Chinese sheets carry their own labels,** for example 酸度 (acidity),
酒精度 (alcohol) and 等级 (classification). Echo's Chinese sheet reads
"靓茨伯酒庄的副牌葡萄酒", i.e. Lynch-Bages's second-label wine.

**Language versions mostly agree, but not always.** 897 groups of PDFs
differ only by a language token. In 11 of them an analytical figure
disagrees:

- Carbonnieux 2025 yield 25 vs 28;
- Prieuré-Lichine yields 32 vs 37.

**Not every disagreement is a translation conflict.** Larcis Ducasse's
Murmure 2018 shows that:

| Document | Upload path | Alcohol |
|---|---|---|
| French sheet | 2020 | 14.5 % |
| French sheet (a later revision) | 2022 | 14 % |
| English sheet | 2022 | 14 % |

The disagreement is between *revisions*, not languages.

**How the model represents each situation:**

| Situation | Representation |
|---|---|
| Same document in several languages | separate Artifacts + a candidate ArtifactRelation LANGUAGE_VERSION_OF |
| A revision | ArtifactRelation REVISION_OF, ordered by ArtifactDate |
| Different documents in different languages | separate Artifacts, no relation |
| Conflicting versions | separate Observations, reconciled; the reconciliation names its rule (D5) |
| Language-specific labels | Observation.raw_label + lang, never translated in place |
| Untranslated observations | the normal case; a Translation is a derived record with its method |
| Canonical text | CanonicalValue.lang |

No equivalence between language versions is ever assumed.

**First-class concepts:**

- **SourceEstateRelationship** (added): OFFICIAL_SITE, GROUP_SITE, SHOP,
  NEGOCIANT_SITE, REGISTER_ENTRY, MENTIONS, with validity. A source is
  never an estate's identity.
- **Multi-estate documents:** Attribution, many per artifact (retained).
- **Multi-estate observations:** Observation.subject_status (added). A
  line in a group document is ESTABLISHED, PROPOSED or UNRESOLVED as to

**Rejected:** a source belonging to every estate it mentions. Vignobles
K's site names Château Le Rey, which is outside the universe; it is
recorded, not adopted.

## 14. Corporate and legal information review

**What the corpus contains.** 115 domains carry legal labels:

- SIRET: 133 observations on 42 sites;
- registered office / head office: 155 on 42+;
- share capital: 45;
- legal form: 18;
- publication director: 79;
- company forms in values: SAS 24, SARL 8, SCEA 5, EARL 5.

**The decision: auxiliary observation (class B), not core estate
identity.** A legal notice describes the *website publisher*, which may
be:

- a group (Vignobles K);
- an operating company;
- a négociant;
- or the estate's own company.

Taking it as the estate's identity would repeat the "source = estate"
error. So:

- the statement is an Observation whose subject is the **Source**
  (case P14, a SIRET);
- a Party may later carry legal identifiers (`Party.identifiers`) once a
  publisher-to-owner link is established;
- nothing in Phase 4 depends on it.

  - rewrite v1–v5 or the discovery data.

**Ingestion requirements for Phase 4, set but not solved here:**

- The raw store (7.7 GB) stays outside git, content-addressed.
- Extraction is lazy and versioned. An Artifact can exist as PENDING or
  NEEDS_OCR with no observations, and the system must operate before
  every document is parsed.
- OCR is a later extraction stage that adds observations under its own
  extractor version. It never replaces the bytes.
- Re-crawling is incremental, using validators and content hashes as in
  Phase 3.3.
- Indexes are built over Artifact, Placement, Observation and
  Attribution.
- DocumentFamilies are derived on demand.
- Disk budget is a first-class constraint: Phase 3.3 had to stop and
  compress to fit.
