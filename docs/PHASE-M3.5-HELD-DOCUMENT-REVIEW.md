## 3. Pilot decision rubric

`tools/m35_rubric.py`, applied to the document's own text. The first rule that decides wins.

- **T1 — title.** The document's first non-empty line is its title when it is a bare name phrase: it opens with château, clos, domaine or cuvée, or is a model wine's whole name, followed by at most a year. A heading ("Le climat") is no title.
- **T2 — exact name.** The title must equal a model wine's name, normalised (accents, case, hyphens, "ch.").
  - A shared word is no match: "Château Braude Fellonneau" is not "Château de Braude".
  - A title naming another model wine, or a wine the model does not list, makes the claim WRONG_EXISTING_ATTRIBUTION.
- **T3 — producer lines.** Lines naming the company, address, telephone, e-mail or site are not identity, and are never read for another wine.
- **T4 — vintage.** The title's year is the vintage, and a claim for another year is wrong.
  - A year in a dated line ("dec 2022", "guide hubert des vins 2017") is a date, never a vintage.
  - Filenames, URLs and metadata are never read.
  - A title without a year leaves the vintage ambiguous.
- **T5 — scope.** No other wine of the estate may be named outside the producer lines, and "l'autre vin" or a second wine in the opening makes the wine ambiguous.
  - A document naming each claimed wine beside the one vintage is a shared sheet (both supported).
- **T6 — colour.** A stated blend must be of the wine's colour.
- **T7 — open decisions.** A wine an open identity decision governs is never attributed (Echo, Lions de Batailley, Hauts de Lynch-Moussas, Haut-Bailly II, Terrasses).
- **Fallback.** A document with no title is judged by M3.4A's rules, unchanged. Their dispositions carry over: ESTATE_VINTAGE_ONLY and IDENTITY_BLOCKED become WINE_AMBIGUOUS, CONFLICT becomes CONFLICT_REMAINS.
- **O1 — no decision, no attribution.** A document never put to the evidence store has no accepted decision, and is not attributed (Part O).
- **Eligible.** A SAFE (or shared) claim is eligible only when an ACCEPTED decision on these exact bytes names exactly that estate, wine, vintage and role, the held file re-hashes, and the artifact's manifest provenance exists.

## 4. False positives caught

- **Accepted for the wrong estate.** 208 pilot claims were ACCEPTED in the evidence store for a wine the document is not:
  - The cause: the guard checked year and second-wine, never the estate. The group's shared media library put every sheet in both estates' folders.
  - Claims by title: chateau braude fellonneau 60, chateau de braude 30, chateau galland 60, chateau mongravey 32, cuvee de mongravey 26.
  - Each is recorded as a correction proposal. No decision was changed.
- **Spurious vintages.** Mongravey's sheets were accepted as Braude **1981**, and Braude's as Mongravey **1984**: years printed in the sheets, not their vintage. T4 refuses them.
- **Dates read as vintages.** 7 accepted claims name a year the document prints only in a dated line, such as a press date. Examples: `Ch-Galland-2011.pdf` claimed as 2015; `Ch-Galland-2014.pdf` claimed as 2017; `Ch-Braude-Fellonneau-2020.pdf` claimed as 2022; `Ch-Galland-2011.pdf` claimed as 2015.
- **Rules the review itself had to correct:**
  - A section heading ("Le climat", on a Laville Haut-Brion sheet) parsed as a title naming an unknown wine. T1 now requires a strong opening word, or a model wine's whole name.
  - The producer-line pattern first matched French prose ("sa") and bottle counts. It now requires company forms, a Gironde postcode before a town, or a full telephone number.
- **M3.4A's refusals still hold.** Estate-wide vintage pages, white sheets under a red wine, *Mondot*, event dates and press digests are re-judged by the same rules. None became eligible (section 7, and `tools/test-m35.py`).
