## Findings
Estate 5 of 61.

**The estate contradicts itself on its own two sheets, and the records
inherit it.** Lynch-Bages 2003 and 2007 both hold the blend
`CS 75, ME 15, CF 8, PV 2` — identical to each other four years apart,
and identical to the vineyard planting in `plantings.js`, which was read
from `Chateau-Lynch-Bages-2007-FR.pdf`.

That French sheet calls the figures **Encépagement** — the planting. The
English sheet for the same vintage calls the same figures **Blending**:

```
FR 2007   Encépagement : 75 % cabernet sauvignon, 15 % merlot,
                         8 % cabernet franc, 2 % petit verdot
EN 2007   Blending : 75% Cabernet Sauvignon, 15% Merlot,
                     8% Cabernet Franc, 2% Petit Verdot
EN 2010   Blending: 79% Cabernet Sauvignon, 18% Merlot,
                    2% Cabernet Franc, 1% Petit Verdot
```

By 2010 the English sheet states something that differs from the
planting, so from then on it is a real assemblage. For 2003 and 2007 two
of three signals say planting — the French label, and one figure serving
two vintages — and the third says the estate published it under
"Blending".

**I have left the figures and recorded the contradiction**, rather than
delete something the estate printed under that heading on thin evidence.
Its other 24 blends vary properly, CS 60–84 and ME 9–31, which is what a
real assemblage looks like.

**One sheet is misnamed by the estate.**
`Chateau-Lynch-Bages-1989-FR.pdf` ends "Classification Grand Cru Classé
**Millésime 1990** Propriétaire Jean-Michel CAZES". The manifest filed it
under 1990, from its contents rather than its name, which is right.
